## What this branch is

An honest assessment of how complete gritmc is, where it stands against SteelMC
and Pumpkin, the probability it ever finishes, and what should change about how
the work runs. Three documents and one instrument:

* **`docs/ASSESSMENT-2026-08-19.md`** is the deliverable. Read it whole once;
  its section 7 is the one-paragraph version if that is all there is time for.
* **`ci/surface-census.py`** is the instrument, with `--selftest`.
* **`docs/FINDINGS.md`** is new, append-only, and seeded. It exists because one
  defect was found five times in one run.

## What a fresh agent most needs to know from it

1. **`ci/surface-census.py` measures from the jar side**, so unlike
   `ci/unported-inventory.py` its ranking is not a ranking of our own
   attention. Use it when the question is "what does vanilla have that we do
   not". It needs `oracle-work` symlinked into the worktree, or `--oracle`.
   Its `named` column is an **upper bound** on implementation and its
   `nowhere` column is a **lower bound** on what is missing. Neither is a
   completeness percentage and the file says so at the top.
2. **Every published floor in this repo is an unclamped floor.**
   `VANILLA_JVM_OPTS=-Dmax.bg.threads=1` determinises a vanilla harvest without
   modifying vanilla at all. One flag, one harvest, no port, and it turns three
   "under floor, no resolving power" rows into real numbers. This is the single
   cheapest high-value measurement available and it has never been taken.
   Assessment 2.3 and 3.7 item 5.
3. **The SteelMC headline needs restating before it is used.** Their reference
   really is a modified vanilla, and they published that themselves in plain
   terms. A launch line implying concealment dies when a reader clicks the
   link. The claim that survives is item 2 above plus the End at 0 against six
   unmodified references.
4. **The speed pillar is contested and unverified.** Their published benchmark
   is 10,201 chunks in 3.98 s on a 9950X. Ours is 225 in 6.55 s on a
   Threadripper 1920X, and the README already records that our generator peaks
   at 1.00 cores. Download their released binary and run their benchmark on our
   box before printing any speed claim. That is running a shipped artefact, not
   reading source, so it stays inside the clean-room rule.
5. **The worldgen dump and the running server are different configurations.**
   The dump places 8 of 20 structure sets; the live server places none. Every
   parity figure comes from the dump.
6. **`docs/SERVER.md` is stale on recipes.** `recipes.rs` loads 1,056 of 1,585.

## What this branch did not do, deliberately

* No build, no `cargo check`, no gate, no recording, no harvest, no supervisor.
* No competitor source was read and no competitor is named in any code or test
  in this tree. `docs/` names them, which is research and not contamination.
* No shared document was edited. `docs/SERVER.md`'s stale recipe line and the
  proposed `unit` and `question` fields on `ci/figures.toml` are **named in
  the assessment and not applied**, because a paused repo with 292 live
  branches is the wrong place to touch files other lanes hold.

## The three changes worth doing first, from section 4

They are ordered by cost, cheapest first, and none of them is a port.

1. **Land `ci/run-detached.sh` on `main`.** It exists, it is falsified with
   seven cases including `kill -9`, and it is stranded on
   `origin/docs-waiter-t2-blindspot`. Empty agent wakes at 615k and 361k tokens
   were the run's largest single waste and this is the fix.
2. **Turn batching on.** 1.725x measured on the real queue, 35.0 h against
   60.4 h, currently disabled. That is 25 hours of gate time per queue of that
   size, already paid for.
3. **Set the lane count from the gate's drain rate, not the usage allowance.**
   A serial gate at 66.1 expected minutes per landed branch absorbs 22 branches
   a day, 38 with batching. Eight to ten lanes produced 198 commits in thirty
   hours while the unlanded backlog grew 1,342 to 1,377. Everything above the
   drain rate is inventory that depreciates as `main` moves.

## What would invalidate the assessment

* **A second human contributor.** Section 3.7 item 1; it is the only lever that
  moves the 10% out of single digits, and it invalidates the throughput half of
  section 4 as well.
* **The speed check going our way.** If our wall figure survives comparison on
  the same box, axis four flips and section 2.7 changes.
* **26.3 shipping.** It resets 32,366 block states, 69 packets, every data
  dump, every golden table and every floor, and there is no port pipeline in
  `ci/`. Any estimate here past three months is soft for that reason.
* **Any of section 3.1's five assumptions failing.** They are listed first
  precisely so the conclusion can be rejected by rejecting one.
